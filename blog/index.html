Cyber Resilience Act

Does the CRA apply to my company? A scope checklist

The Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) applies to products with digital elements, meaning software or hardware and their remote data processing, that connect to a device or network and are made available on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity, paid or free. Where the manufacturer is based doesn't matter. Pure SaaS is generally out; the cloud backend a product needs is in. Medical devices, vehicles, certified aviation products, marine equipment and defense products are excluded.

Cyber Resilience Act

CRA 24-hour vulnerability reporting for non-EU manufacturers

Since September 11, 2026, Article 14 of the Cyber Resilience Act requires every manufacturer of a product with digital elements sold in the EU, wherever it is based, to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents through ENISA's single reporting platform: an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours, then a final report. A manufacturer with no EU establishment reports to the CSIRT picked by Article 14(7): authorised representative first, then importer, distributor, and where most users are.

DORA

Answering a DORA vendor questionnaire as a US SaaS company

DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) has applied since January 17, 2025 to EU financial entities, not to their vendors. But Articles 28 to 30 make your client assess you, record your contract in a register of information, plan its exit, and put minimum clauses in your contract. The questionnaire is how it checks. Statements rarely close it: dated evidence does, such as a timed restore test, a real export of one client's data and a subcontractor register.
