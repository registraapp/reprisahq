Three dates that matter

All from Article 71 of the regulation. The first one is already behind us.

June 11, 2026

Conformity assessment bodies

The rules on notifying conformity assessment bodies (Chapter IV) started to apply, so notified bodies can be designated ahead of 2027.

September 11, 2026

Reporting is live

Article 14 applies. Actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents go through ENISA's single reporting platform. This also covers products you placed on the market years ago (Article 69(3)).

December 11, 2027

Everything else

Essential requirements of Annex I, SBOM, vulnerability handling, security updates for the support period, technical documentation, conformity assessment and CE marking.

The reporting clock

The clock starts when you become aware of the exploitation or the incident, not when you finish investigating it.

What is different when you are outside the EU

The obligations are the same as for an EU manufacturer. The mechanics are not.

Which CSIRT gets your report

Without a main establishment in the EU, Article 14(7) sets the order: the member state of your authorised representative, then of your importer, then of your distributor, then the one where most of your users are. Knowing this in advance saves hours on the day.

An authorised representative is optional

Article 18 says a manufacturer may appoint one by written mandate. It is not mandatory. If you appoint one, it can hold your declaration of conformity and technical documentation for market surveillance authorities, but it cannot take over your design and vulnerability handling duties.

Your importers will ask for proof

Under Article 19, an EU importer must check that you carried out the conformity assessment, drew up the technical documentation and applied the CE marking. Expect those requests to reach you well before December 2027.

Penalties apply to you too

Article 64 caps fines for breaching Annex I or Articles 13 and 14 at EUR 15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Micro and small enterprises cannot be fined for missing the 24-hour early warning alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Cyber Resilience Act apply if we have no office in the EU?

Yes, if you place a product with digital elements on the EU market. The CRA covers products made available in the EU, wherever the manufacturer is based. Since September 11, 2026, Article 14 reporting applies, including to products placed on the market before December 11, 2027.

Do we have to appoint an EU authorised representative?

No. Article 18 says a manufacturer may appoint an authorised representative by written mandate. It is optional. If you have one, its member state comes first when Article 14(7) decides which national CSIRT receives your reports.

What are the CRA reporting deadlines?

For an actively exploited vulnerability: early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware, notification within 72 hours, final report no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available. For a severe incident: 24 hours, 72 hours, and a final report within one month of the 72-hour notification. All through ENISA's single reporting platform.

Is SaaS covered by the CRA?

A pure SaaS service is generally not a product under the CRA. Remote data processing that a product needs to perform one of its functions, such as the cloud backend of a connected device or app, is part of the product.

What are the penalties under the CRA?

Article 64 sets maximum fines. Breaching the essential requirements of Annex I or the obligations in Articles 13 and 14 can cost up to EUR 15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Micro and small enterprises cannot be fined for missing the 24-hour early-warning deadline alone, but they still have to report.

Is Reprisa a law firm or a notified body?

No. Reprisa builds the process, the tooling and the documents. For legal interpretation, use counsel. For products that need third-party conformity assessment, the notified body does the assessment; the technical file we prepare is the input.
